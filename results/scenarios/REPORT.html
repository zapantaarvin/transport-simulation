Six sites: do all files finish within 1 min and 5 min?

Six made-up sites, each with 50,000 file requests over October. For each site the question is: which settings get every file delivered within 1 minute, and within 5 minutes, of its request?

How settings are written
4 @ 200
4 threads (files moving at once), each up to 200 MB/s
Max
1000
the site's total pipe in MB/s, shared by all threads
Pass ✓
100% on time
every one of the 50,000 files made the limit
Fail
even 1 late
99.9% on time is still a fail
Synthetic trafficThese sites are invented from verbal descriptions of real traffic patterns, not measured data. The method carries over to real exports; the numbers don't. New to NAPM? Read module 0 of the study guide first.

How to read the curve chart (site D)

Blue = the most transfers running at once in each hour. Red dashed line = how many the pipe can run at full speed (800 ÷ 500 = 1.6). The curve pokes above it at the nightly batch and month-end, so a guarantee isn't possible, but the simulator shows 2 threads @ 500 still gets every file in within 5 min: files can wait a little and still make it.

Curve charts for the other sites




A

Site A: 24h, week 1 busier, very random

max rate too small

Observations

  • Average load is only 2%, but random spikes hit 157% of max for 5 minutes (mostly week 1).
  • No setting gets every file in on time. Best: 64 @ 500 → 5 min 99.51% (242 late), 1 min 97.59%.
  • The late files cluster on Oct 5 at 4:00 and Oct 7 at 0:00, the biggest spikes.

Tuning

  • Max ×1.5 (1500) → 4 @ 500 passes 5 min (1 min: 19 late).
  • Max ×2 (2000) → 4 @ 500 passes both.
  • Threads alone can't fix it.
After-run: heatmap, queue, latency (at 64 @ 500)


B

Site B: 2-day burst, then mellowing

max rate too small

Observations

  • The burst runs at 246% of max for an hour (304% over 5 min), right at the start of the month.
  • The grid plateaus at about 83%: past 8 threads @ 100, nothing improves. That's the signature of a pipe that's too small.
  • Oct 1 has only about half its files on time; the rest of the month is about 100%.

Tuning

  • Max ×3 (1500) → 4 @ 500 passes 5 min. ×4 (2000) passes both.
  • Or spread the burst: if it's backfill, pace it over more hours.
After-run: heatmap, queue, latency


C

Site C: office hours

easy for 5 min

Observations

  • Busiest hour 17%, busiest 5 min 46%. Never near the pipe.
  • 5 min passes from 1 @ 100 up.
  • 1 min passes only at 1 @ 300, one thread using the whole pipe. At 2 @ 300, 9 files are late: two threads split the 300 MB/s, so a 15 GB file slows down.

Tuning

  • 5 min: 2–4 threads @ 100 (the extras are for redundancy).
  • 1 min: one thread at full pipe passes, but one stuck file blocks everything. A bigger max rate is safer.

D

Site D: nightly batch + month-end surge

5 min yes, 1 min needs more pipe

Observations

  • Daily batch spikes at 1:00, and the month-end surge pushes past max (127% over 5 min).
  • 5 min passes at 2 @ 500 and 4 @ 200 / 300 / 500. At 8+ threads it drops to 99.9% (4 files late at 8 @ 100): too many threads split the pipe.
  • 1 min tops out at 98.91%: files are bigger here (median 500 MB).

Tuning

  • 5 min: 4 threads @ 200. Don't go above 4.
  • 1 min: max ×1.5 (1200) → 4 @ 300 passes both.
After-run: heatmap, queue, latency (at 2 @ 500)


E

Site E: steady, 90% small + 10% huge

easy for 5 min

Observations

  • Light load (busiest hour 10%). 5 min passes from 1 @ 200 or 2 @ 100.
  • 1 min misses by a handful: 2 @ 300 has 7 late files out of 50,000 (99.986%). A 15 GB file at 300 MB/s takes 50s alone, so any wait pushes it over.
  • Checked outside the grid: 1 @ 400 (the whole pipe) passes; 2 @ 400 has 1 late.

Tuning

  • 5 min: 2–4 threads @ 100.
  • 1 min: per-thread rate is the knob, not threads. Only one full-pipe thread passes here, so a bigger pipe is the practical fix.

F

Site F: outages, backlog rush

max rate too small at rushes

Observations

  • 26 of 30 days are perfect. The Oct 22 (9.7 h) and Oct 27 (9.2 h) outages cause rushes at up to 471% of max.
  • Grid plateaus at 97.09% (5 min): the same pipe-too-small signature as B.

Tuning

  • Max ×3 (1800) → 8 @ 500 passes 5 min. 1 min still fails even at ×4 (99.34%).
  • Better: pace the backlog after an outage instead of releasing it all at once.
  • Confirm what your request time means after an outage (arrival vs original creation).
